# Limitations, Epistemic Boundaries, and AI-Contribution Disclosure

## 1. Real vs. Metaphor & What Is Not Claimed

- **Not externally peer reviewed:** All three manuscripts in `papers/` are unreviewed research preprints. Passing Lean 4 kernel checks certifies the logical validity of the formalized statements inside Lean — not external referee acceptance, historical priority, or statement-to-literature fidelity.
- **No quantum hardware measurements:** Moonlab (`third_party/moonlab/`) is a classical state-vector simulator compiled to WebAssembly; `quantum_rng` (`third_party/quantum_rng/`) is a seeded classical pseudorandom generator; QGTL (`third_party/qgtl/`) provides classical differential-geometry C kernels. Nothing in this repository is a physical experiment on quantum hardware or certified quantum randomness.
- **Prior work & unreviewed preprint citations:**
  - The spectral floor inequality, its Kramers variant and Conjecture E stem from unreviewed Zenodo preprints by B. W. Jonah (`doi:10.5281/zenodo.23538829`, `23510634`, `23524332`). The floors are Jonah's prior results; this repository formalizes them for arbitrary supports. Jonah's preprint `23538829` also states an attainment criterion for closed families that overlaps `saturation_tfae`. Jonah's computer-assisted note `23510634` (20 October 2026) already claimed the exact values $d(W(7,2)) = 1575$ and $d(Q^+(9,2)) = 6975$, which imply the kernel-checked lower bounds $1455$ and $6444$ proved here. The Conjecture E source is AI-assisted and AI-refereed; it already proves cases C1 and C6.
  - Symplectic polar-space degrees: Muller et al. (`arXiv:2407.02928`). The repeated-disjoint-copy question: A. Muller and M. Saniga (`arXiv:2609.19862v1`, an unreviewed arXiv v1 preprint). Their Sec. 4.2 gives the context-count product formula $|HS(G_1 \cup \dots \cup G_k)| = \prod_i (|HS(G_i)|+1) - 1$ used in Track 2. A. Cabello (`arXiv:1002.3135`, 2010) reports a related growth result, in which the violation grows exponentially with $n$.
  - $d(Q^+(7,2))=315$ is due to Li, Gupta, and Ganesh (`arXiv:2610.26465`); the numerical five-qubit flat state is due to Cieśliński (`arXiv:2610.07172`); the scalar skewness bound is Okumura (1974).
  - Concurrent independent work: a Zenodo deposit (`doi:10.5281/zenodo.24567331`, created 26 November 2026, after the v3.0.0 CI run) describes a floor-type lower bound, the $1/2$ limit, an exactness criterion for pure states with constant-modulus Pauli expectations, a five-qubit configuration of degree 480, and a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization. It is later than, not prior to, this release; the overlap has not been compared in detail.
  - Novelty claims (Track 1 Section 5 flat states and $|\Psi_{3m}\rangle$, Track 1 Conjecture E formalization, Track 2 arbitrary-$k$ closed forms) rest on bounded literature searches and do not claim exhaustive historical priority. The question answered by $|\Psi_{3m}\rangle$ was posed in this repository's own manuscript, not by another author.
- **Scope of the answers to external questions:**
  - Track 2 answers the stacking-limit question at the end of the second paragraph of Muller & Saniga, Sec. 6, for disjoint Peres–Mermin squares, Mermin pentagrams, mixed square/pentagram unions (`mixed_square_pentagram_minimum`), and arbitrary finite contextual signed seeds (`general_seed_epsilon_tends_to_one`, Theorem 5), all formalized in Lean 4.35.0. Explicit single-seed Pauli coordinates for two doily copies or other non-square/pentagram geometries named in the same sentence are not constructed here (though Theorem 5 applies once any finite contextual signed seed is given). The source poses this as an open question in its discussion, not as a conjecture.
  - Track 1 proves strict-law failure for one explicit representative of each of the nine vertex cases of Conjecture E, for every $N \ge 4$. Case membership of each representative and transfer to whole cases (Jonah's Theorem B4) are outside Lean (Section 2).
